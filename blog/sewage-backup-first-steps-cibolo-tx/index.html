Sewage Backup in Cibolo, TX: What to Do First at Home

Sewage coming up through a floor drain, tub or toilet is a health hazard and a plumbing emergency. Our line in Cibolo, TX is answered 24/7. While you wait for help, stop all water use, keep people and pets out of the affected area and do not flush.

This guide covers the safe first steps, how to work out whether the clog is in your line or the public main, who to call, and what to do about cleanup and insurance.

The first steps when sewage backs up

Act in a calm order. Each step either limits the spill, protects people or keeps a record for later.

1

Stop all water use

Turn off the dishwasher and washer and stop using sinks, showers and tubs. Every gallon you send down adds to the overflow.

2

Do not flush

Put the lid down on every toilet. A flush sends waste to the lowest opening in the house, which is often a tub, shower or floor drain.

3

Keep people and pets away

Close doors to the wet room. Children, pets and anyone with a weak immune system should stay out of the area.

4

Avoid contact

Do not wade through it barefoot. If you must cross the area, wear waterproof boots and gloves, and wash hands thoroughly afterward.

5

Ventilate if safe

Open windows and run fans only in rooms that are dry and away from the spill.

6

Photograph everything

Take photos or video of the water line, damaged items and the drains before you move anything. Your insurer will ask.

Do not mix chemicals. Bleach combined with ammonia or an acidic cleaner can release toxic gas, and a bleach pour on top of a drain cleaner can do the same. Skip the bleach for now. Disinfecting comes after the line is open and the water is gone.

Turn off power only to the affected area, and only if the breaker is dry and you can reach it without standing in water. If water is around outlets, a panel or appliances, stay out and let a professional or the electric provider handle it.

Is it your line or the public main?

The answer decides who fixes it and who pays, so work it out as early as you can. The house line, called the building sewer, runs from the house to the point where it joins the public main. The public main runs under the street and serves many homes.

Look for clues. If every drain in the house is slow or backing up, a toilet gurgles when the washer drains, or a lower floor drain or tub is the first place water appears, the main line is involved. That could be your section or the public one.

Ask the neighbors. If the neighbors have the same problem, the blockage is probably in the main. Look for a manhole in the street or easement, and see whether it is overflowing. If it is, do not approach it, and report it to the utility immediately.

Who to call in Cibolo

Sewer billing in Cibolo changed in November 2025. According to the City of Cibolo, the City no longer bills sewer, and Green Valley Special Utility District (GVSUD) adds the sewer charge. That is why GVSUD is the number to know for a sewer problem in this area.

GVSUD lists an emergency line for suspected sewer clogs at (830) 914-2331. Its main line is (830) 914-2330. Call the emergency number if several homes seem affected, if a manhole is overflowing, or if you are not sure whether the clog is in the street. A utility can check its own main and tell you whether the blockage is on its side.

If the utility finds that its main is clear, or your home is the only one affected, call a plumber. Our 24/7 line sends a licensed plumber to work on the pipes on your side of the property line. A plumber cannot see inside the public main, and the utility handles its own pipes.

What the plumber does to clear a sewage backup

The first job is access. The plumber locates the cleanout, a capped pipe outside or in the garage or basement area that gives a direct path to the main line. If there is no cleanout, access may come from a toilet or a roof vent, which takes longer and is less clean.

Next, the plumber clears the blockage with a cable machine. A drum machine with a heavy cable and a cutting head breaks up a plug or cuts roots. When grease or heavy buildup coats the pipe wall, high pressure water can scour the line, which is the job of hydro jetting. The point of a cable is to open a hole, and the point of a jet is to clean the whole wall.

Then a camera goes in. A video camera on a flexible rod shows the cause, whether that is roots, a sag, a broken joint or a collapsed section. Without a camera, a cleared line is a guess. If the camera finds damage, the next step is repair, described on the sewer line repair page, and you decide with the price in hand.

Honest limits matter. A cable cannot fix a broken pipe, and clearing roots without fixing the break buys time. A plumber cannot see behind a wall or under a slab without opening it, and cleanup of the contaminated space is not plumbing work.

Cleanup and insurance, honestly

After the line is open, the mess remains. Sewage soaks into carpet, drywall, insulation and cabinets, and it carries bacteria. Cleanup and drying belong to a restoration or biohazard cleanup company, which is a separate trade from plumbing. A plumber clears the line and does not clean the house.

Do not rush to throw things out before you have photographed them. Porous items such as carpet pad and drywall touched by sewage are usually removed, and a restoration company knows what must go. Wash hands often, and keep children and pets away until the area is cleaned and dried.

Call your insurer early. Many homeowners policies cover sudden and accidental water damage, but sewer backup coverage often requires an endorsement, a separate add-on. Ask what yours covers before you assume, keep the plumber's invoice and your photos, and ask whether a deductible applies. A utility's responsibility, if its main was at fault, is a separate question to raise with the utility.

What not to do

A few common reactions make things worse. Do not keep running water to see whether it drains. Do not pour chemical drain openers into a backed-up line, because they rarely clear a full blockage and leave dangerous standing water. Do not use a household vacuum on standing sewage, since it can spray contaminated water and wreck the motor.

Do not flush toilets, do not use a plunger over a floor drain full of sewage, and do not step into standing water near electrical outlets. If you smell natural gas, leave the building, call 911 and the gas utility from a safe distance, and do not use switches or phones inside.

Call a licensed plumber right away if sewage is rising, if the smell makes you ill, or if water reaches finished floors. Stay out of the wet area until it is safe. If anyone has symptoms after contact, such as vomiting or fever, call a medical professional.
