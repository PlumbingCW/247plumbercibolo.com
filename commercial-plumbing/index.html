Commercial plumbing in Cibolo, TX and who it serves

Commercial plumbing in Cibolo, TX covers plumbing in buildings that are used for business rather than as a single-family home. That includes restaurants, shops, offices, clinics, daycare centers, gyms, apartment buildings, duplexes and rental houses owned by a landlord. It also covers the property managers who look after those buildings for someone else.

The systems differ from a house in size and use. A business may have more fixtures on one line, bigger water heaters, floor drains and grease handling, and a water meter that matters more because everything there is billed. Restrooms may be used by hundreds of people in a week instead of a family of four.

The population of Cibolo grew from 3,035 in 2000 to 39,019 in the July 2025 Census estimate. A city that size has many businesses and rental properties that depend on working plumbing, and a failure at one of them hits more than one household.

When a business plumbing emergency cannot wait

These are the situations that call for the line right away. Water is pouring from a pipe or a ceiling. A floor drain is overflowing in a kitchen or a restroom. Sewage is coming back up through a floor drain or toilet. A water heater is leaking in a closet that holds inventory. Any of these can close a business for the day and ruin stock, flooring and drywall.

The line is open nights, weekends and holidays, and a licensed plumber is dispatched for same-day appointments when available. We do not promise an arrival time, but we do tell you the price first.

Fire sprinkler systems, HVAC and electrical work are not part of what we do. If the water is coming from a sprinkler head or a fire main, shut off what you can reach safely and call the fire department or the company that services the sprinkler system. If the leaking pipe is only a plumbing line, call us.

First steps for an after-hours leak or flood

The first goal is to stop the water, and the second is to protect what is in the building. Find the main valve for the building or the suite, which is often near the meter, in a utility room or in the mechanical closet. Turn it clockwise until it stops. If you do not know where it is, call and the dispatcher can talk you through the likely places.

Next, move stock and equipment out of the wet area. Lift boxes onto shelves or tables, unplug small equipment only if the floor near the outlet is dry, and keep staff away from wet floors near electrical gear. Do not enter a room where the water is touching a live panel.

Take photos or short videos of the damage and the source before anything is moved if you can do it safely. Your landlord or insurer will want them, and so will anyone settling who pays for the repair.

Floor drains, lift pumps and grease

Floor drains in a kitchen or mechanical room carry wash water, mop water and the occasional overflow to the sewer. They fill with grease and food waste over time, and a floor drain that smells or drains slowly is warning you before it backs up. A plumber can clear the line with a cable or high pressure water, as described under high pressure water cleaning.

Many food businesses also have a grease trap or interceptor. It is a tank that slows wastewater so fat and oil can float to the top and stay out of the sewer. When the trap is full, the line behind it backs up into sinks and floor drains.

Here is the honest limit. Pumping out a grease trap is a separate hauling service, not a repair. If you need a trap pumped or cleaned, ask the dispatcher, who can tell you whether a plumber can help or whether you need a hauler. We can clear the lines that feed and follow the trap, and we can tell you whether the trap itself looks damaged.

Lift pumps and sump basins are different again. Where a restroom or floor drain sits below the sewer line, a pump lifts the wastewater up. If the pump fails or the float sticks, the basin overflows, and the plumber checks the pump, the float and the discharge line.

Backflow prevention and why businesses have it

A backflow assembly is a valve device that keeps water from flowing backward into the public supply. Many commercial properties have one on the main supply or on irrigation or fire lines, because a business can have chemicals or equipment that could contaminate water if pressure dropped.

Testing rules vary, so ask your water utility and the City of Cibolo building department what applies. Ask the dispatcher whether a plumber can test or replace the assembly, and the plumber can explain what the paperwork usually involves.

If an assembly leaks, is damaged, or has been removed, call. A plumber can replace the assembly and explain what the utility will ask for before work starts.

Water heaters and tankless units for commercial loads

Commercial water heaters have a harder job than home units. A restaurant needs a lot of hot water for dishes and a hand sink, and an office may need only a small amount for restrooms. The right type depends on how much hot water is used at the busiest time and how quickly it is needed.

Tank units store hot water and recover slowly after a rush. Tankless units heat on demand and can save space, but a large demand needs a large gas line or electrical supply. Planned work is explained on the tankless water heater page, and the plumber can talk through sizing for the kitchen or restroom load.

Some commercial units need special venting, drain pans and expansion control, and heater work in a leased space often needs permission first. Permit rules vary, so ask the City of Cibolo building department before replacing a unit.

Planning work around business hours

Not every job is an emergency. A plumber can plan repairs, fixture replacements and drain jetting for after closing or early morning so customers are not walking over open floors. Water may need to be off for part of the job, so the plan includes when it goes off, how long it stays off, and how restrooms are handled meanwhile.

Say so on the call. A dispatcher can set up a time that fits your hours. For work that can wait, a planned visit after closing is often less disruptive than a same-day slot in the middle of service.

Restaurants and shops also benefit from a walk-through of shutoff valves. Knowing where each one is before a leak happens saves minutes when it matters.

Tenant or landlord, who is responsible

Responsibility depends on the lease. In many leases the landlord handles the main building lines and the tenant handles fixtures inside the suite, but that is a general pattern, not a rule. A leak inside a wall may be one party's job and a clogged sink drain the other's. Check your lease before authorizing repair work, and call your landlord or property manager as soon as you can.

If you manage rental properties, it helps to give the dispatcher the property address, the tenant contact, and who approves the price. Tell the dispatcher if an insurer or a landlord needs specific details on the plumber's invoice, so the paperwork is right the first time.

The City of Cibolo enforces the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), and a plumber should be able to explain how a repair meets it. Check any plumber's license through the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners (TSBPE) online search, and ask for the Responsible Master Plumber's number on the plumber's invoice.
