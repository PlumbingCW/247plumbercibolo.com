What drain cleaning actually does in Cibolo, TX

Drain cleaning is the removal of a blockage from a fixture drain, a branch line or the main line. The plumber uses a hand auger, a motorized cable or high pressure water, then runs a flow test to prove the pipe is open. It is different from a plunger fix, because a plunger moves a clog back and forth while a cable or jet breaks it apart or pulls it out.

Most home drains are small. A kitchen sink drain is usually 1.5 inches across, a tub or shower drain 1.5 to 2 inches, a toilet drain about 3 inches and the main sewer line commonly 4 inches. The smaller the pipe, the less buildup it takes to close it, which is why a kitchen sink stops long before a main line does.

A clog also has a cause. Grease that cooled in a trap, hair wound around a pop-up stopper, wipes that never broke down and tree roots in an old clay line all look like "a slow drain" from the sink. They need different tools, so the first job is working out which one you have.

Where the clog is, based on what backs up

The pattern of what is slow or gurgling points to the location of the clog before anyone picks up a tool. One slow fixture means a clog in that fixture's own branch. Several fixtures on the same floor mean the branch they share. Every drain in the house, or a floor drain bubbling when you flush, means the main line.

Kitchen sinks: why they clog again

Kitchen drains are the repeat offenders. Cooking fat leaves the pan as a liquid, cools inside the trap and the horizontal branch, and coats the pipe wall in a layer that catches coffee grounds, rice and food scraps. The clog grows from the walls inward, so the drain slows for weeks before it stops.

A hand auger or cable removes the plug, but it often leaves the greasy wall layer behind. If the same sink clogs every few months, a high pressure water jet can scour the pipe wall so the buildup does not restart so quickly. The hydro jetting page explains how that works and when it is worth the cost.

Garbage disposals add a second problem. A disposal grinds food into small pieces, but it does not dissolve them, so starch, coffee grounds and eggshell can settle in the outlet pipe. A jammed or humming disposal is a different repair, covered on the disposal repair page.

How a plumber clears the line

A typical visit follows a fixed order. The plumber confirms which fixtures are affected, removes and inspects the trap where that is possible and then chooses the method. A small sink machine with a thin cable handles most sink and tub branches. A heavier drum machine with a larger cable reaches the main line through a cleanout, and a camera confirms what the cable hit.

Cutting heads are matched to the blockage. A spear or drop head punctures a soft plug, a blade or chain head scrapes grease off the wall and a root cutter shears roots in clay or older cast iron. After the pipe is open, the plumber runs water for several minutes to confirm it drains freely, because a pipe can pass a quick test and still be half full of debris.

Honest limits matter here. A cable cannot repair a collapsed or offset pipe, and clearing roots without fixing the break only buys time. If the camera shows a broken section, the next step is a repair, described on the sewer line repair page, and you decide with the price in hand.

What should never go down a drain

Wipes are the biggest cause of main line clogs, including wipes labeled flushable. They do not break up the way toilet paper does, and they snag on rough joints and roots. Grease, coffee grounds, rice, pasta and produce stickers belong in the trash. In bathrooms, hair is the main culprit, followed by cotton swabs and dental floss.

Chemical drain cleaners deserve a plain warning. Caustic products can sit in a fully blocked pipe for hours, which can soften old pipe and joints and turns the standing water into a splash hazard when a plumber opens the line. If you already used one, tell the dispatcher so the plumber wears the right protection.

Drains and the sewer in Cibolo

Sewer billing in Cibolo changed in November 2025. According to the City, it no longer bills sewer, and Green Valley Special Utility District (GVSUD) adds the sewer charge. That matters in practice because a blockage that backs up every drain at once may be in your own line or in the public sewer main in the street. GVSUD lists an emergency line for suspected sewer clogs at (830) 914-2331.

If a neighbor has the same symptoms, or the manhole near your home is overflowing, call the utility first. If only your home is affected, a plumber can use a camera and the cleanout to tell which side of the property line the clog is on. Either way, the question to settle first is who owns the pipe, because that decides who pays for the fix.

The City of Cibolo enforces the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), which sets how traps, vents and cleanouts are installed. A repair that changes the drain layout should follow it, and permit rules vary, so ask the City of Cibolo building department before work that opens walls or floors.
