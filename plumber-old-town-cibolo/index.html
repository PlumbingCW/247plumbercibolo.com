What Old Town Cibolo is

City documents from the downtown revitalization packet call Old Town Cibolo the historic core. There is an Old Town zoning district around Main Street, with the railroad alongside it and Town Creek running through. The packet also notes five existing homesteads, an existing single-family neighborhood to the west and the Old High School, known as the White Building.

Calls from Old Town go to the same line that serves the rest of Cibolo, TX plumbing, so there is no separate number to look up.

Homes and businesses on the same blocks

According to Community Impact in May 2023, Old Town sits in council districts 2 and 7, and the City added a commercial overlay district there in 2010. On March 28, 2023, council voted 4 to 1 to rezone so that residential properties that asked could be single-family only.

Of 93 properties in the rezoning area, 12 asked for medium-density single-family, 47 asked to stay commercial or retail, 21 did not respond and 13 were already commercial. The result is a street where one block can be mostly homes and the next mostly storefronts.

Before you call, know whether your building is a home, a business or both, and who owns the pipes. A tenant, a landlord and a property manager each need to hear about a leak.

If your building predates modern plumbing

Historic core only means older buildings may exist. The age of buildings in Old Town is not documented, so the date your own building was built matters more than its address.

If your house or shop predates the 1970s, ask whether the drains are original. Cast iron drains were common in mid-century construction and can scale on the inside, which narrows the pipe. If slow drains keep returning, a camera look through a sewer inspection shows what is inside before anyone digs.

If a drain is simply clogged, clearing the drain with a cable or jet is the usual first step. If you have a tank water heater past roughly 8 to 12 years old, a small leak at the base often means a repair visit will not be enough, and the plumber will say so.

If you run a shop or office, restrooms and drain lines see heavier daily use than a home does, and commercial plumbing covers that work.

Permits, utilities and who to ask

The City of Cibolo enforces the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC). Permit rules vary, so ask the City of Cibolo building department before any work that opens walls or floors. City Hall is at 200 S. Main St.

Two utilities serve Cibolo, the City of Cibolo and Green Valley Special Utility District (GVSUD), depending on the address. Since November 2025 the City no longer bills sewer, and GVSUD adds the sewer charge. Check the name on your bill to learn who handles the street side of your meter.
