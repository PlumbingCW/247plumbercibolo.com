Before we arrive

What to do while you wait for an emergency plumber

1

Shut off the main water

Turn the main valve clockwise until it stops. Most homes have it near the meter or where the supply line enters the house.

2

Stop using drains

If a drain is backing up, do not flush, run the dishwasher or start the washer until the line is clear.

3

Keep power away from water

Stay out of standing water near outlets, and shut off power at the panel only if the floor around it is dry.

4

Call and describe it

Tell the dispatcher what is leaking or backing up, so the right plumber and equipment are sent.

Why we answer all night

A 24/7 emergency plumber for a fast-growing city

Cibolo has grown quickly. The population was 3,035 in 2000 and 32,276 in the 2020 Census, and the Census Bureau's July 2025 estimate is 39,019. Census estimates for 2020 to 2024 show 82.3 percent of homes owner-occupied and a median home value of $351,900. For most households a plumbing failure is the homeowner's own repair bill, so how fast the water is stopped decides how big that bill gets.

That is why the line runs 24/7. A supply line that fails at 2 a.m. can soak drywall and flooring before sunrise. An emergency plumber who answers at that hour can talk you through the shutoff, and the damage stops at the valve instead of the floor.

Water damage is also the reason we ask the first question on every call: where is the water coming from? If it is coming from a fixture, the fixture's own shutoff valve stops it in seconds. If it is coming from a wall, a ceiling or the floor, the main valve is the only fast fix, and the plumber sorts out the rest once the water has stopped.

Not every call is an emergency, and we say so when it is not. A dripping faucet or a slow tub can wait for a same-day appointment when one is available. A rising toilet, a spreading leak, a gas smell or no water at all cannot. For a gas smell, leave the building and call 911 and your gas utility from a safe distance before you call us.

Local details that help

Cibolo plumbing notes worth knowing

Two water providers

The City of Cibolo and Green Valley SUD both serve Cibolo. The name on your bill tells you who handles the street side of your meter, and who to call about a main break in the road.

Spotting a leak early

City water customers can use My Water Advisor. A leak drop icon appears when the meter has not idled for 3 hours in a row during a day. If you see it, turn off every tap and call about leak detection.

Check any plumber

Ask for the Responsible Master Plumber's license number, which begins with an M, and look it up in the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners search. Ask for proof of insurance too.

Know your shutoff

In February 2021 the City issued a boil water notice for its Upper Pressure Zone after pressure dropped. A notice like that is separate from a break inside your home, but finding your main shutoff before you need it helps in both cases.
