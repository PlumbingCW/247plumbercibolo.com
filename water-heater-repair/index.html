Water heater repair in Cibolo, TX: what can be fixed

Water heater repair in Cibolo, TX covers the parts around a tank, not the tank itself. Thermostats, heating elements, igniters, thermocouples, valves and fittings can be replaced. The steel tank that holds the water cannot be patched once it corrodes through. That single fact decides most repair calls.

A tank water heater is a fairly simple machine. Cold water enters through a dip tube that sends it to the bottom, where a gas burner or electric elements heat it. Hot water leaves from the top. A relief valve protects the tank from over-pressure or over-temperature, and a drain valve at the bottom lets the tank be emptied.

When one of those parts fails, you notice a symptom. The first job of the plumber is to match the symptom to the part, then confirm it with a test, not just replace parts until it works.

Water heater leaking: where the water comes from

Where the water appears tells you a lot. Look carefully before you decide the tank has failed, because several leaks are repairable.

A tank body leak cannot be repaired because the steel liner is the container. Once rust has made a hole, patching only moves the leak. It also usually means the tank is near the end of its life. If you see water pooling steadily at the base, close the cold supply valve and call.

A steady drip from the discharge pipe is a safety signal. That pipe exists so a failing valve has somewhere safe to send hot water or steam. Do not cap it, plug it or ignore it.

Water heater emergency: when it cannot wait

Treat it as an emergency if water is spreading across a floor or the unit is leaking near electrical equipment. The same goes for loud banging with hot water spurting from the discharge pipe. Gas smell: leave first

If you smell gas near the water heater, do not flip switches, light anything or troubleshoot. Leave the building, then call 911 and the gas utility from a safe distance. We never promise a response time for a gas leak, and a gas smell takes priority over any plumbing question. Do not go back inside until the utility or the fire department says it is safe.

No hot water on an electric heater

An electric tank has two heating elements and two thermostats, one upper and one lower. The top thermostat controls the upper element first, then passes power to the lower one after the top of the tank is hot.

Start with the breaker. A tripped double breaker is a common reason for a cold tank. If it trips again, there is a short in an element or the wiring. Do not keep resetting it.

Next is the high-limit reset button, a small red button behind the upper access panel. It trips if the water gets too hot. Cut power at the breaker before you open any panel. A tripped high-limit is a symptom, not a cause, so a bad thermostat or element is often behind it.

If the upper element fails, there is no hot water at all. If only the lower element fails, you get a small amount of hot water that runs out quickly. A plumber tests each element and thermostat with a meter and replaces the one that reads open.

No hot water on a gas heater

A gas tank needs a flame, and the flame needs a sensor to prove it. On older units a standing pilot light stays lit, and a thermocouple generates a small current to prove the pilot is on. If the thermocouple wears out, the gas control valve shuts the gas off, and the pilot will not stay lit.

Newer units use an electronic igniter and a flame sensor, which show error patterns on a light. A dirty flame sensor or a failed igniter can stop the burner from running. The gas control valve, the thermostat in the same housing, can also fail, and that is a larger repair than a sensor.

Relighting a pilot is something many homeowners do, but if the pilot will not stay lit or you smell gas at any point, stop. A gas unit with a flame problem is a job for a licensed plumber, not a tinkering project.

Lukewarm water, short showers and noisy tanks

Lukewarm water has a few causes. On an electric tank, a failed lower element is the leading suspect. On any tank, a broken dip tube can send cold incoming water to the top, where it mixes with the hot water and weakens the supply. A thermostat set too low also gives lukewarm water, so check the setting first.

Short hot water, where the shower turns cold after a few minutes, often means sediment. Minerals settle at the bottom of the tank and take up space, so the tank holds less hot water. On a gas unit, sediment also insulates the water from the burner and wastes fuel.

Popping and rumbling noises come from the same cause. Water trapped under a layer of sediment boils into steam bubbles that burst, creating the knocking sound. Flushing the tank can help if the tank is not too far gone. A heavily scaled tank may not recover.

Rusty or discolored water from the hot tap only, and not the cold, points to the tank interior or the anode rod. The anode rod is a sacrificial metal rod that corrodes in place of the tank walls. Replacing it can extend tank life if it is done before the lining is eaten away.

Safety around a water heater

A water heater holds energy, so treat it with care. Four rules matter most.

  • Never block, cap or plug the relief valve or its discharge pipe. Without it, a failing thermostat can build pressure the tank cannot hold.
  • Scalding water is a real risk. Water around 140 degrees Fahrenheit can scald in seconds, and 120 is a common safer setting, especially with children and older adults in the home. Check the thermostat setting.
  • Turn off power at the breaker before opening an electric heater's access panels.
  • If you smell gas, leave and call 911 and the gas utility from a safe distance.

Repair or replace: the general rule

Tank water heaters often last roughly 8 to 12 years, which is general trade knowledge and not a promise about any one unit. A heater under that age with a failed part is usually worth repairing. A heater at the upper end of that range that leaks from the tank is not.

Replacing a thermostat, element, igniter or valve is a small job compared with a new heater.

If the tank is old, scaled and showing rust, spending on a repair can mean paying twice within a year. Ask the plumber what else is likely to go next and what the replacement price would be. When replacement is the better call, the replacement page explains the steps. If you want to compare, a tankless unit is another option that comes with its own trade-offs.

What to do before the plumber arrives

1

Shut off the cold supply

Close the valve on the cold pipe above the heater by turning it clockwise. This stops the tank from refilling and limits the water on the floor.

2

Cut the power or gas

Turn off the breaker for an electric unit if the floor near the panel is dry. Set a gas unit to off only if it is safe, and never relight it yourself after a leak.

3

Protect the area

Move boxes and stored items away and lay towels or a wet vac at the base. Leave the floor drain, if any, clear.

4

Take a photo of the label

The rating plate on the side shows the model, age and fuel type, which helps the plumber plan parts.

Water in Cibolo and what it does to a tank

Cibolo gets nearly all its water from Canyon Regional Water Authority, which blends Wells Ranch groundwater with Lake Dunlap surface water. According to the 2025 consumer confidence reports, the City reported chlorine residual averages of 2.26 ppm for the Wells Ranch supply and 1.70 ppm for the Schertz supply, and GVSUD reported 1.68 ppm. Those reports do not give a hardness figure.

That matters for your tank because sediment buildup depends on mineral content, and we cannot tell you what it is at your address. Test your tap, and ask your water utility for its annual water quality report.
