Drains that keep slowing in an older house

If your house is older and the drains slow down again within a few months of being cleared, the pipe may be the problem. Cast iron builds up rough scale inside, and the scale catches paper, grease and hair. Old clay or fiber pipe in a yard line can let roots in at the joints. A cable can open the pipe, and a camera can show why it keeps closing.

That is where clearing the line and a camera look belong together. The cable removes the plug, and the camera shows whether the pipe is scaled, offset, cracked or full of roots. The plumber explains what is on the screen and what the repair options are before anything else happens.

Honest limits apply. A camera shows the inside of the pipe, not what is under it, and it cannot tell you the age of the pipe by sight alone. If the video shows a crushed section, the next step is a repair and a price you approve first.

Repiping and pinhole leaks

Galvanized steel supply lines are the classic problem in older houses. Zinc coating wears off, rust builds up on the inside and the pipe narrows until water barely trickles from a tap. Copper can also develop pinhole leaks where the wall thins. If you see brown water, low pressure at several fixtures or damp spots that return after a patch, the supply system may be at the end of its life.

A full repipe job replaces the old supply with new pipe, and a plumber can tell you whether a few sections or the whole house makes sense. Repiping is planned work, so it is not an emergency job, but a pipe that is actively leaking is. The line is open for problems that cannot wait.

Water inside a wall or under the floor is harder to find. Electronic leak detection can narrow a hidden leak to a small area, which means a smaller hole in the wall and a smaller repair.

2
