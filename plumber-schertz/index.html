Schertz began as a railroad stop and grew into a city

Schertz is named for Sebastian Schertz, and the community took shape along the Galveston, Harrisburg and San Antonio Railroad, which arrived in 1876. The post office opened in 1884 under the name Cut Off and was renamed Schertz in 1899. The city was incorporated in 1958 and counted 18,694 residents in 2000. By 2015 that number was 36,244.

That history is background. The age of your own house matters more, and part of the first job on any call is working out what the plumber is dealing with, from the water heater age to the drain layout.

The city also spans three counties, Guadalupe, Bexar and Comal. For a repair call that is a reminder to check your water bill before assuming who serves you. The bill names the utility that handles your meter, and that is the number to call if the problem is on the street side of it.

What we get called about in a growing city

Where a house was built in the last twenty years, the failures tend to be fittings and appliances rather than whole pipe runs. Supply line connectors under sinks let go, and toilet fill valves fail.

Tank water heaters near the end of their life start to weep at the base. A small leak at the drain valve or a failed relief valve can often be fixed with a water heater repair, while a leaking tank cannot.

In any older home, the pipes behind the walls and under the floor matter more. Cast iron drains were common in mid-century construction and can crack or scale on the inside. That narrows the pipe until a visit for clearing the drain turns into a camera inspection.

We do not have a count of Schertz homes by age, so the fair test is simple. If your house is more than about forty years old and the drains keep slowing, ask for a camera look.

Hidden leaks are the quiet problem in both groups. A water meter that moves when every tap is off, a warm spot on a floor or a bill that jumped for no reason all point to water escaping somewhere you cannot see. Electronic leak detection finds the spot without tearing out a whole room.
