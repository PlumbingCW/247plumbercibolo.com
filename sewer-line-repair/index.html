Sewer line repair in Cibolo, TX: when a clog is really a break

Sewer line repair in Cibolo, TX is the fix for a pipe that is cracked, collapsed, sagging or invaded by roots, not just clogged. A clog can be cleared with a cable or jet. A damaged pipe will clog again unless the pipe is repaired. The difference matters, because clearing a broken line over and over costs more than fixing it once.

Your sewer line runs from the house to the public main, usually under the yard. Gravity moves the waste, so the pipe must slope downhill all the way. Anything that creates a low spot, a gap or a rough edge gives solids a place to stop. Over time a small snag grows into a full blockage.

If you do not yet know what is wrong, cleaning the line is often the first step, and a camera shows whether cleaning is enough or a repair is needed.

Sewer backup: what to do first

Treat a sewage backup as an emergency. Wastewater carries bacteria, and it ruins flooring and drywall fast. Stop using sinks, tubs, the washer and the dishwasher, and do not flush. Water you add has only one place to go.

Keep people and pets away from sewage. Wear gloves and boots if you must step in it, and keep the area ventilated. Turn off power to any room with standing sewage near outlets only if you can reach the breaker without stepping through it.

Our line is open nights, weekends and holidays, and a licensed plumber is dispatched for same-day appointments when available. Waiting until morning is a poor choice if sewage is rising in a tub or a floor drain.

Signs the sewer line is damaged

Some signs are easy to spot, and others build slowly. A pattern across several fixtures is more telling than one slow sink.

Multiple drains back up together

The shower gurgles when the washer drains, or a toilet bubbles when you run a sink. The shared main line is restricted.

Gurgling and bubbling sounds

Air is being forced back through traps because water cannot move down the pipe.

Sewage smell indoors or in the yard

A crack or open joint lets sewer gas escape, or a dry trap lets it in.

A lush, wet patch in the yard

Wastewater leaking from a broken pipe feeds the grass and keeps the soil soft.

Drains that slow down again soon after clearing

Roots, a sag or a break keeps catching new debris.

Settling soil or a sunken spot

Water washing soil into a broken pipe can leave a void and a dip above it.

Do not confuse a sewer smell in one bathroom with a line failure. A dry P-trap in a rarely used sink or floor drain is a common source and needs only water in the trap. A smell that follows a backup or appears in several rooms points to the line.

How a camera inspection finds the problem

A sewer camera is a small waterproof lens on a flexible cable that is fed into the line through a cleanout. The plumber watches a live view and can record it. The camera shows what the pipe actually looks like, which beats guessing from symptoms.

What the camera can show:

  • A belly, which is a low spot where the pipe has sagged and water sits
  • An offset joint, where two sections no longer line up
  • A crack or break in the pipe wall
  • Root intrusion, where fine roots have entered through a joint
  • A collapse, where the pipe has caved in and the camera cannot pass

A locator at the surface can then mark where the camera head is, so the plumber knows how deep the pipe is and where to dig if it comes to that. The full process is described under sewer camera inspection.

Cameras have limits. A heavily blocked line may need cleaning before the camera can pass. A collapse can stop the camera, so the pipe beyond it stays unseen. Debris and water can obscure a view. Murky images do not prove a pipe is fine, and the plumber should say so when the view is poor.

Repair options and their trade-offs

There is no best method for every line. The right choice depends on the type of damage, the depth, what sits above the pipe and the condition of the rest of the line.

Sewer line repair options
MethodHow it worksWhere it does not fit
Spot repair by diggingThe plumber excavates to the damaged section, cuts it out and replaces it with new pipeDeep lines, lines under a driveway, slab or mature tree make it costly and disruptive
Cured-in-place pipe liningA resin-soaked liner is pulled into the old pipe and cured to form a new pipe inside itA collapsed, badly offset or heavily bellied pipe, and lines with sharp bends may not accept a liner
Pipe burstingA cone is pulled through the old pipe, breaking it outward while pulling in a new pipeNeeds entry and exit pits, and may not suit lines near other buried utilities or in unstable ground

Digging is the most direct option and is often the right one for a single bad joint. It shows you the pipe, and the repair is easy to check. It also disturbs the yard, and the plumber has to restore the surface.

Trenchless methods reduce digging, which helps when the line runs under a slab, a driveway or landscaping you want to keep. They need a pipe that is clear enough to line or burst, and they do not fix a bad slope. Ask the plumber why a method fits your line, not just what it is called.

Lining a pipe that runs under a slab is a request that comes up often. It is possible in some cases, depending on the pipe and the damage, and the plumber can say after a camera inspection.

Cleanouts: the access point

A cleanout is a capped pipe that gives direct access to the sewer line. It is often found outside near the house, or inside a garage or utility area. Removing the cap lets a plumber run a cable, a jet or a camera into the line without going through a toilet.

If your home has no accessible cleanout, the plumber may have to pull a toilet or cut access, and installing a cleanout can be part of a repair. Do not open a cleanout when the line is full of sewage unless a plumber advises it, because pressure can send waste out. Keep the area around it clear.

Who owns which part of the line

The line has two parts. The lateral from your house to the property line is generally the homeowner's. The public main in the street belongs to the utility. Where exactly the dividing point sits can vary, so confirm with your utility before assuming either way.

According to the City of Cibolo, since November 2025 the City no longer bills sewer. Green Valley SUD (GVSUD) adds the sewer charge. GVSUD lists an emergency line for suspected sewer clogs at (830) 914-2331, and its main line is (830) 914-2330. If neighbors have the same backup, or a manhole near your home is overflowing, call the utility first, because the problem may be in the public main.

If only your home is affected, a plumber can use the camera and the cleanout to tell which side of the property line the problem is on. Settling that question first decides who pays.

Permits, utility locates and insurance

Permit rules vary, so ask the City of Cibolo building department before any digging or pipe replacement. The City enforces the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), which sets how drains, traps and vents are installed.

Underground gas, electric, water and communication lines can run near a sewer line, and hitting one is a danger and an expense. This is general safety knowledge. Do not start digging on your own, and ask the plumber or your utility about having buried lines marked.

Insurance varies. Many homeowners policies do not cover wear, root damage or a deteriorated pipe, and sewer backup is often an endorsement you have to add. Ask your insurer what applies before assuming either way, and keep photos of any damage.
